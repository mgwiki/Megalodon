# Vampire to Megalodon Proof Reconstruction: Expert Review Report

Date: 2026-07-18

Repositories:

- Megalodon: `/project/Megalodon`, branch `vampire/megalodon5`
- Vampire fork: `/project/vampire-leancheck`, branch `vampire/megalodon5`

This report is an updated expert handoff for the Vampire-to-Megalodon proof
reconstruction project. It takes into account the earlier daily reports, the
audit reports through `audit-REPORT-2026-07-16.md`, the audit responses, the
Prover9/Ivy analysis, the design documents written on July 16 and July 17, the
Smolka-style transformation discussion, and the current state of the local
branches on July 18.

The main conclusion is deliberately conservative:

```text
The original project goal is not yet achieved.
```

The project has moved substantially toward the right architecture. Vampire now
emits a much richer Megalodon-oriented certificate, and Megalodon now checks
native `Syntax.pf` proof terms for a growing kernel and preprocessing fragment.
The work has also moved away from the original Python-heavy proof
reconstructor as a qualifying path.

However, the system still does not reconstruct 100 representative original
Megalodon theorems from THF Vampire proofs inside the original Megalodon
development context. It also still contains too much prototype logic in the
large Megalodon OCaml importer and in the large Vampire exporter. The audits
were correct that the project must converge toward a small Prover9/Ivy-style
certificate calculus, with complex Vampire inferences expanded by Vampire into
simple proof objects and checked natively by Megalodon.

The most important recent progress is:

- strict native live mode no longer treats absence of a usable certificate as
  success;
- certificate-derived dynamic `Known` facts are no longer allowed on counted
  proof-term paths by default;
- source obligations are tracked and audited more explicitly;
- generated `set` and local set-reflexivity obligations have a native
  reflexivity route;
- live source-context composition now resolves some local hypotheses and local
  definition structure;
- the focused line-172 theorem reconstructs in the original live context;
- Skolem/CPS reconstruction has progressed from leaking certificate-local
  choice witnesses to replacing those witnesses by proof-term-level epsilon
  abstractions;
- an uncommitted July 18 experiment shows that the representative line-183
  Skolem/CPS candidate can discharge certificate-local witnesses, moving the
  immediate blocker to final proof-of-proposition and live goal composition.

The most important remaining failures are:

- the line-183 representative theorem still does not reconstruct as an E1
  proof in the committed branch;
- the line-183 uncommitted experiment is not clean enough to commit and still
  fails later in final proof composition;
- Skolemization is not generally solved, especially for explicit substitution
  mismatches such as the line-233 family;
- FOOL proposition equality-to-truth conversions still leak through as
  brittle proof-of-proposition obligations in live composition;
- the Vampire-side primitive IR is not yet cleanly factored;
- the Megalodon-side checker is still a large monolithic prototype;
- there is no current passing 100-theorem E1 test suite.

## Target

The target remains:

```text
100 representative original Megalodon theorems, exported as THF, proved by
Vampire with a per-problem timeout no greater than 10 seconds, reconstructed
into Megalodon proof terms, and checked by Megalodon in the original source
context, with no aby, no admit, no incomplete QED, no hidden bridge premise, no
certificate-local trusted theorem, and no Python proof reconstruction on the
qualifying path.
```

The qualifying path must use Megalodon's own proof checking. It may use
classical logic because the development assumes classical logic and `xm`
is available. It should use the Megalodon library's logical constants and
equality rather than private replacements.

## Evidence Classification

The earlier reports and audits converged on the following evidence labels.
They are important because broad pass counts were previously easy to
overinterpret.

```text
E1  Original-context proof reconstruction.
    A proof term is checked inside the original Megalodon development and
    proves the current theorem goal. This is the project target.

E2  Exported-THF-bound closed proof reconstruction.
    A native Megalodon proof term checks a Vampire certificate against the
    exported THF/source problem, but the result is not yet composed back into
    the original source theorem position.

E3  Primitive-kernel proof-term checking.
    Synthetic or focused live certificates exercise native proof-term
    elaboration for the small clausal or preprocessing kernel.

E4  Structural and diagnostic integration evidence.
    Vampire emits certificates; Megalodon parses them, validates structure,
    source maps, and metadata, or runs transitional diagnostics. This is useful
    but is not proof reconstruction.
```

The only final success class is E1. E2 and E3 are valuable milestones. E4 is
valuable engineering evidence but must not be counted as proof reconstruction.

## Audit History and Response

The audits made several strong criticisms. They were largely correct.

The July 12 audit found that the original Python/script path was not a
credible final reconstruction architecture. It generated useful skeletons but
used `admit`, `aby`, bridge assumptions, and library-specific heuristic search.
That path could produce Megalodon files that checked only because the theorem
had been strengthened with extra assumptions. The correct response was to
quarantine Python from the qualifying path.

The July 13 audit reinforced the Prover9/Ivy direction. The important lesson
from Prover9 is that compact prover inferences are expanded by the prover into
a small proof-object vocabulary. Prover9's `prooftrans` lowers complex
justifications into simple steps, and Ivy checks a small resolution and
paramodulation-oriented calculus. The lesson is not that Megalodon must adopt
Ivy syntax. The lesson is that selected literals, substitutions, rewrite
positions, equality orientations, and clause transformations should be
exported by Vampire while Vampire still has exact internal information.
Megalodon should not rediscover those details by proof search.

The July 14 audit identified hidden extra premises in "strict" mode. Strict
mode then meant certificate structural validation, not proof reconstruction.
The response introduced closed modes, source-map formula checking, stronger
source-origin checks, and explicit rejection of bridges and derived premises
in counted modes.

The July 15 audit accepted that closed textual results were more meaningful,
but warned that the implementation was still growing in the wrong place. It
criticized the large textual emitter, arity-specific helper lemmas, and lack
of native `Syntax.pf` checking. The response moved work toward native proof
terms inside Megalodon.

The July 16 audit found the most serious trust-boundary issue in the native
preprocessing frontier: the checker could dynamically add the proposition it
wanted as a `Known` theorem and then use it. That is a hidden bridge
assumption. The response on `vampire/megalodon5` was to fail closed by default
when counted native paths would require certificate-derived `Known` facts.
The old behavior remains available only behind
`MEGALODON_CERT_ALLOW_TRANSITIONAL_PREPROCESS_KNOWN=1` for structural
diagnostics. Such runs are E4 only.

The current branch therefore reflects the audit direction better than earlier
branches, but it has not completed the architectural reset. The importer and
exporter are still too large, and the current work still risks becoming a
Megalodon-side replay engine rather than a small checker for Vampire-emitted
primitive proof objects.

## Overall Architecture

The intended final architecture is:

```text
Megalodon source theorem and proof context
  -> THF export with source and origin metadata
  -> Vampire proof search, timeout <= 10 seconds
  -> Vampire-side primitive proof expansion
  -> versioned S-expression certificate
  -> Megalodon parser and source resolver
  -> native Syntax.pf elaborator for each certificate step
  -> proof of refutation
  -> source-context composition with the original goal
  -> checked Megalodon proof of the original theorem
```

The pieces that exist today:

- THF export from Megalodon exists and is used by live `vampireaby` calls.
- Vampire emits a native S-expression certificate with much richer metadata
  than the initial TPTP proof output.
- Megalodon parses that certificate and validates source-map and primitive
  metadata.
- A native proof-term checker exists for a growing clausal kernel fragment.
- Several preprocessing transformations now have native proof-term support.
- Live `vampireaby` can call Vampire, parse the certificate, and attempt
  strict native reconstruction in the current Megalodon context.
- Some focused live examples, most importantly line 172 of
  `examples/hammer/100thms_12_h.mg`, reconstruct.

The pieces that remain incomplete:

- the Vampire certificate side is not yet factored into a small typed
  `MegalodonKernelStep` builder;
- the Megalodon checker is not yet factored into small modules with narrow
  specifications;
- source-context composition is incomplete for general local sections,
  local definitions, generated set facts, and conjecture negation;
- Smolka-style preprocessing is only partially proof-producing;
- Skolemization has focused successes but remains a major unsolved family;
- no broad E1 suite passes.

## What Has Been Implemented

### Native Certificate Format

Vampire now emits a Megalodon-oriented S-expression certificate. The
certificate contains source entries, source names, roles, formula records,
step identifiers, parent identifiers, step rules, primitive metadata, selected
literals, substitutions, rectification maps, Skolem records, split
dependencies, AVATAR records, and other rule-specific data.

This is a major improvement over the initial approach. It means the final
system can be driven by exact data from Vampire instead of by guessing from
pretty-printed proof output.

The format is still too broad and too stringly typed. The long-term target
should be a small typed IR with explicit constructors and a corresponding
Megalodon elaborator. The S-expression surface can stay, but it should encode
small proof objects, not arbitrary snapshots of Vampire internal state.

### Native Megalodon Proof-Term Checking

`src/vampire_cert_v1.ml` now constructs real `Syntax.pf` terms for a growing
fragment and checks them with Megalodon's `check_propofpf`/`extr_propofpf`
interfaces. This is materially different from the old textual script emitter.

The current proof-term support includes a clausal kernel seed and selected
preprocessing proof constructors. It includes focused coverage for resolution,
factoring, equality resolution, equality factoring, substitution,
paramodulation-like reasoning, URR primitive chains, AVATAR component and
restricted SAT-resolution replay, direct Skolemization, and several FOOL/CNF
or source-boundary cases.

The important qualification is that this is still not a complete E1 result.
Some fixtures are synthetic. Some checks are exported-problem checks rather
than original-context theorem proofs. Some broad preprocessing runs remain
structural diagnostics rather than counted proof reconstruction.

### Trust-Boundary Reset

After the July 16 audit, counted native paths were changed so they do not
silently install certificate-derived propositions as `Known` theorems. The old
transitional behavior now requires:

```text
MEGALODON_CERT_ALLOW_TRANSITIONAL_PREPROCESS_KNOWN=1
```

and is classified only as E4 structural evidence.

The native proof-term checker also traverses proof terms and rejects generated
certificate-derived proof constants whose names begin with `vampire_`, except
for explicitly reviewed fixed logical basis constants. This is a partial guard
against accidentally reintroducing hidden bridge premises.

### Source-Obligation and Source-Context Work

The source resolver now distinguishes known global facts, local hypotheses,
local definitions, generated facts, conjectures, negated conjectures, and
unresolved obligations. It also has source-obligation audit counters for
formula-checked, unsupported, missing, equality-checked, set-reflexivity, and
true-checked obligations.

This matters because the final proof must be linked to the original Megalodon
development, not merely to TPTP labels. The final system needs proof-linked
sources:

```text
source name -> original Megalodon proposition -> proof or local variable
```

Current source linking has real improvements:

- hash-backed known facts can be resolved in the loaded context;
- local hypotheses can be matched through local definition offsets;
- generated set-reflexivity obligations can be discharged by reflexivity;
- source-map presence is separated from actual dependency on the empty-clause
  proof path;
- conjecture and negated-conjecture entries are handled at final
  refutation-to-goal composition rather than by global lookup.

The gap is that source-context composition is still not general enough. The
line-183 theorem demonstrates this clearly.

### Live Logical Basis Transport

Commit `17e9e05` made important progress in transporting the certificate's
logical basis into the live Megalodon context. The certificate's internal
proofs use normalized principles such as double-negation elimination,
not-forall/existence, `False` elimination, and proposition extensionality. In
an E1 proof those cannot remain certificate-private facts. They have to be
expanded through the actual facts available in the current Megalodon file.

The live-basis work expands double-negation elimination through loaded `xm`,
uses the loaded `not` and `False` structure, expands not-forall/existence
principles, and chooses appropriate `prop_ext` facts from the live delta.

This was enough to make the representative line-172 live theorem reconstruct
after earlier failures.

### Source-Linked Skolem/CPS Work

Commit `59bf8d7` added source-linked Skolem/CPS reconstruction machinery. It
recognizes CPS-shaped propositions, tries supplied refutation paths for
negated conjectures, applies guided suffixes for local proposition binders,
recognizes source bindings for negated conjectures, and traverses some
Skolem/CPS replay through conjunction and disjunction contexts.

Commit `1cb9100` then changed the Skolem/CPS path to skip candidates that
still contain local choice witnesses. This was a trust-boundary correction:
candidate proof terms that mention certificate-local choice witnesses should
not be accepted as final proof terms.

Commit `d35a81f` added proof-term-level replacement of exact witness terms.
For existential CPS steps, it constructs epsilon witness terms and replaces
those exact terms by the current bound witness under the appropriate binder
depth. This abstracts away Skolem/CPS epsilon witnesses from the proof term
instead of leaking certificate-local choice constants.

The committed verification for `d35a81f` was:

That is E1-style focused evidence for that theorem.

## Source Linking Status

The source-linking story is better than it was, but still incomplete.

The final system needs to connect each Vampire source to one of:

- a global Megalodon theorem, axiom, definition, or previously loaded fact;
- a local proof variable;
- a local definition;
- a generated fact such as a set-command equality;
- the conjecture or negated conjecture associated with the current theorem
  goal.

Set-command facts deserve special treatment. Some TPTP axioms come from
Megalodon `set` commands and have no corresponding user-written claim. In
the common equality/reflexivity case, they should be proved directly in
Megalodon, usually by definitional conversion or reflexivity. The current
source resolver has a native generated/set-reflexivity route, and the smoke
suite covers this class.

The current unresolved source-linking problem is final composition with the
current theorem goal. The line-183 example is:

```text
Section PropN.
Variable P1 P2 P3:prop.

Theorem and3I : P1 -> P2 -> P3 -> P1 /\ P2 /\ P3.
aby.
Qed.
```

Vampire sees a closed exported theorem over proposition variables. The live
Megalodon goal is under local section variables. The native certificate can
produce refutation/CPS propositions over exported variables. The final E1
operation must instantiate the exported universal binders with the live local
variables and prove that the resulting target is convertible to the current
goal.

The current code has several pieces of this, but not a clean operation. The
right abstraction should be:

```ocaml
instantiate_conjecture_to_goal :
  live_context ->
  current_goal ->
  certificate_proof ->
  certificate_proposition ->
  negated_conjecture_binding ->
  instantiated_proof * instantiated_proposition * instantiated_target
```

The key check should be:

```text
instantiated_target converts to current_goal in the live Megalodon delta
```

After that check, Megalodon should apply the appropriate classical
refutation, double-negation, or CPS eliminator. This should be a guided
source-context operation, not a broad enumeration of candidate proposition
terms.

The line-183 debug history shows that a convertible target exists after
instantiating the exported proposition variables with the live local variables.
Therefore the remaining source-linking failure is not an inability to recover
original names like `P1`, `P2`, and `P3`. It is a binder/proof-term
instantiation and composition problem.

## Equality and Logical Constants

The user asked why there is a private equality rather than directly using
Megalodon's equality. The concern is valid.

The certificate format and importer still have internal representations such
as `vampire_eq_prop`, `vampire_true`, and `vampire_false` for exported FOOL
and TPTP-level formulas. Such internal names can be useful as a parsing and
normalization boundary. They should not become private trusted equality or
logic in the final proof.

The final E1 proof must use Megalodon's own equality and logical constants
from the loaded library/context:

- equality;
- `False`;
- `not`;
- `or`;
- `and`;
- `iff`;
- `exists`;
- `forall`;
- `prop_ext`;
- `xm`;
- false elimination and related classical facts.

The live-basis work is a partial answer. It expands several certificate-basis
principles through live Megalodon facts. The line-183 uncommitted experiment
shows the next equality/logic problem: a final proof-of-proposition candidate
expects an `iff A true` proof while the constructed subproof has only an
implication `A -> true`, or vice versa. This suggests that the FOOL
proposition-equality-to-true bridge needs a systematic live-basis proof
constructor rather than ad hoc proof application variants.

Existing native helpers such as `native_core_true_eq_from_proof` and
`native_core_proof_from_true_eq` are likely relevant. The fix should not
introduce a private equality. It should transport between:

```text
proof of A
proof of A -> true
proof of true -> A
proof of iff A true
proof of Megalodon equality/prop-ext form
```

using the live Megalodon logical basis.

## Skolemization Status

Skolemization remains one of the main unfinished Smolka-style transformations.

Progress made:

- Vampire emits Skolem symbols, dependency variables, source formulas,
  result formulas, and substitutions.
- Megalodon checks some direct Skolemization proof terms.
- The focused line-172 theorem passes with the current committed witness
  abstraction work.
- The line-183 experiment shows that a CPS Skolem replay candidate can
  discharge certificate-local witnesses if generated propositions are closed
  under the witness-replacement binder depth.

Remaining problems:

- explicit Skolem substitution mismatches still occur in other cases, such
  as the line-233 family;
- direct and CPS Skolem proofs are not yet specified as a small independent
  macro certificate;
- some proof terms still risk leaking epsilon/choice details unless the
  abstraction logic is applied at the correct binder depth;
- final live composition after Skolem/CPS replay still fails for line 183;
- the current implementation is too embedded in the monolithic importer.

The desired Skolem macro certificate should include:

```text
source formula
existential binder position
dependency context
introduced symbol declarations
explicit substitution
result formula
proof strategy: direct choice or CPS
```

Vampire should emit this from its internal Skolemization data. Megalodon should
check it by constructing proof terms using the live classical/choice basis and
by verifying that the result formula is exactly the source formula transformed
by the explicit substitution.

Negative tests are essential here. A certificate with the wrong Skolem result
formula must fail before any final theorem proof is attempted.

## Smolka-Style Transformations

The Smolka-style lesson is not "reimplement all of Vampire preprocessing in
Megalodon." The useful lesson is:

```text
Preserve the proof DAG and source correspondence, and justify each
preprocessing edge by a proof-producing transformation in the target system.
```

For this project, that means:

- Vampire should emit explicit fine-grained transformation records;
- Megalodon should check each record as a small proof-term macro;
- unsupported transformations should fail closed in counted modes;
- broad normalization by guesswork should remain diagnostic only.

The relevant transformation families are:

- source entry binding and definition expansion;
- rectification and alpha-renaming;
- lambda/de Bruijn normalization;
- FOOL boolean/proposition transformations;
- ENNF and negation movement;
- CNF and literal projection;
- predicate definition introduction and folding;
- Skolemization;
- AVATAR split generation and split refutation;
- set-command definitional equalities.

Current status:

- some formula/source-map transformations are structurally checked;
- several core and preprocessing fixtures now produce native proof terms;
- rectification metadata is emitted, including variable maps;
- predicate definition and definition-input cases have guarded support;
- AVATAR component and restricted SAT-resolution fixtures pass in focused
  native proof-term checks;
- Skolemization has focused direct and CPS support but is incomplete.

The next step should be to extract transformation checkers into a separate
module with a narrow API. That module should be fed by Vampire-emitted
primitive transformation objects. It should not keep growing as a collection
of local pattern matches inside `vampire_cert_v1.ml`.

## Prover9/Ivy Direction

The project is partly on the Prover9/Ivy path, but not far enough.

What matches the recommendation:

- the preferred surface is now S-expressions rather than JSON;
- Python is not a qualifying proof reconstruction path;
- Vampire emits much more explicit proof metadata;
- Megalodon constructs native proof terms for supported fragments;
- dynamic bridge/known assumptions have been reclassified and gated;
- design documents now state a small kernel direction.

What still does not match:

- Vampire has not yet normalized its proof into a clean small typed
  certificate calculus;
- macro inferences such as URR, superposition variants, demodulation, AVATAR,
  and preprocessing are often represented by rich special records rather than
  by lowered primitive sequences;
- the Megalodon importer still contains broad proof construction logic;
- the small kernel is not yet physically separated enough for review;
- milestones still tend to follow benchmark-driven failures.

The recommended primitive kernel remains close to:

```text
input/source boundary
rename or alpha conversion
explicit substitution/instantiation
binary resolution
factoring
equality resolution
equality factoring
paramodulation
reflexive/definitional simplification
empty-clause contradiction marker
```

Rules such as superposition, demodulation, URR, SAT subsumption, and some
AVATAR explanations should normally be expanded by Vampire into this small
kernel or into a separately specified small propositional certificate layer.

- use `/project/tmp` for temporary artifacts;
- do not run Vampire for more than 10 seconds per problem;
- export problems in batches;
- run Vampire in parallel;
- check outputs in parallel;

```text

  small positive/negative certificate fixtures, seconds
```

Only T4 E1 passes should count toward the final 100 theorem goal.

## Current Blockers

### Blocker 1: Line 183 Final Composition

Line 183 remains the active representative live-context blocker. The committed
branch fails before full E1 reconstruction. The uncommitted experiment moves
past Skolem/CPS witness discharge but fails later in proof-of-prop conversion.

The current failure shape includes:

```text
expected: ((iff A) true)
actual:   A -> true
```

and the reverse orientation in other candidate variants. It also reports
certificate-only Skolem symbols in rejected proof-of-proposition expansions.

The likely fix is a systematic live-basis conversion between proof of a
proposition, equality/iff to true, and true-to-proposition forms. This should
be implemented generally, not as a line-183 special case.

### Blocker 2: Source-Context Instantiation

The final theorem goal is live under Megalodon local binders. Vampire's
certificate is closed over exported variables. The current code still relies
too much on candidate enumeration.

The required operation is guided instantiation of exported conjecture binders
against the current live goal, with proof and proposition transformed in
lockstep.

### Blocker 3: Skolemization Macro

Skolemization needs a specified macro certificate and negative tests. The
current direct and CPS support is useful but still embedded and incomplete.
The line-233 family indicates that explicit Skolem substitution checking is
not yet robust.

### Blocker 4: Monolithic Implementation

The key Megalodon file is still very large. The Vampire exporter is also very
large. Expert review will be difficult until the core checker is extracted
into small modules with clear interfaces and invariants.

### Blocker 5: Proof-Object Normalization on Vampire Side

Vampire still emits too many broad inference-specific records. The next
Vampire-side work should be a real primitive builder that lowers macro
inferences before Megalodon sees them.

## Detailed Chronology

This section gives the expert reviewer a chronological view of how the project
arrived at the current state. It is intentionally explicit because the same
words, such as "strict", "closed", "source-linked", and "native", meant
different things at different times in the project.

### Phase 1: Python Skeletons and Textual Megalodon Scripts

The earliest implementation path downloaded the Vampire and Megalodon
repositories and started from Vampire proof output that could be parsed
outside Vampire. A large Python script translated Vampire/TPTP proof material
into Megalodon script skeletons. That script recovered source names,
generated claims, inserted proof steps where it could, and used admitted
claims or `aby` where it could not complete the proof.

This phase was useful for exploration. It established that:

- Megalodon could export many `aby` obligations as THF;
- Vampire could solve many of those obligations under a small timeout;
- source names could often be recovered from mangled TPTP names;
- the proof objects needed much more information than ordinary TPTP output
  provided;
- the human-readable Megalodon `claim` style from files such as
  `examples/form100/SepE` was a useful debugging target.

However, this phase was not a credible final proof reconstruction path. The
Python script kept growing into an independent proof-search engine. It had
library-specific patterns, bounded reconstruction search, equality-chain
search, proof skeleton generation, and ad hoc treatment of Megalodon library
theorems. Even if the final generated proof were checked by Megalodon, that
direction would not converge: each new Vampire inference or library theorem
shape caused another Python-side search rule.

The user repeatedly objected to that direction. The audits later confirmed
the objection. Python remains useful for corpus analysis and logs, but it must
not construct proof terms on the qualifying path.

### Phase 2: Rich Vampire Export and JSON/Outline Experiments

The next phase modified Vampire to print richer data. The exporter emitted
more details about inference rules, parent clauses, substitutions, literals,
and generated names. This was motivated by a correct observation: Vampire
knows exactly which inference was applied, which literals were selected, which
substitution was used, and which result was produced. Megalodon should not
have to rediscover those facts.

This phase still had a design problem. The exporter was printing a growing
collection of details needed by the current Python or textual importer. It
was not yet a small proof-object calculus. JSON and outline records helped
debugging, but the architecture still encouraged this cycle:

```text
failure in importer
  -> inspect current Vampire internals
  -> add another exported field
  -> add another Python or OCaml recognizer
  -> pass one more benchmark family
```

That cycle discovered important data requirements, but it was not a stable
design.

### Phase 3: Closed Textual Certificate v1

The project then introduced a certificate v1 path in Megalodon. The early
version still generated textual Megalodon scripts, but it added important
trust-boundary controls:

- source maps were checked;
- bridge assumptions were counted;
- closed mode rejected bridge premises;
- source formulas were compared against exported THF declarations for a
  supported fragment;
- generated proof scripts were checked for `admit`, `aby`, and incomplete QED;
- committed certificate corpora were added for reproducibility.

This was a real improvement. The July 15 audit accepted that this phase
produced meaningful closed textual evidence, at least for supported fragments.
It also identified the next problem: textual generation and arity-specific
helper lemmas were still the wrong final representation. The importer should
construct `Syntax.pf` proof terms directly.

### Phase 4: Native `Syntax.pf` Core

The next phase moved key work into Megalodon's native OCaml proof-term
representation. The checker began constructing `Syntax.pf` and checking it
with Megalodon's proof checker rather than only writing scripts. This is the
most important technical pivot in the project.

The native core path created a small seed for first-order clausal reasoning:
resolution, substitution, factoring, equality reasoning, paramodulation-like
proofs, and contradiction. It also started to resolve source proofs from the
loaded Megalodon context.

The July 16 audit confirmed that this core seed was genuine progress. It also
found that a broader preprocessing path had recreated bridge assumptions in a
hidden way by dynamically installing certificate-derived propositions into
the known theorem table. That forced the current trust-boundary reset.

### Phase 5: Trust Reset and Source Context

On `vampire/megalodon5`, certificate-derived dynamic `Known` facts became
fail-closed by default. Transitional known insertion is now opt-in and E4
only. This was necessary for any future proof-term pass count to mean
anything.

The same period added more original-context source resolution:

- global known facts are checked through hashes and the loaded context;
- source obligation audits report formula/equality/reflexivity classes;
- generated set-reflexivity facts can produce native reflexivity proofs;
- local hypotheses can be matched even in the presence of local definitions;
- strict live native mode fails if it cannot use a Vampire certificate.

This was the first credible path toward the actual E1 target.

### Phase 6: Live Basis and Skolem/CPS Frontier

The most recent phase focused on using checked Vampire certificates to build
proofs of the actual current Megalodon theorem. The representative successes
and failures are line 172 and line 183 in `examples/hammer/100thms_12_h.mg`.

Line 172 became a focused live success after transporting certificate-private
logical basis facts into the live Megalodon basis. This mattered because
principles such as double-negation elimination must come from the loaded
library, especially from `xm`, not from a private certificate theorem.

Line 183 became the active blocker. It combines local proposition variables,
conjecture instantiation, FOOL proposition equality, Skolem/CPS preprocessing,
and final proof-of-proposition composition. Work on this case has been
instructive because it exposes exactly the source-context and Smolka-style
issues that will recur in larger proofs.

## Subsystem Status

The following status table is a practical map of the project. It separates
what is implemented, what is trusted, what is only diagnostic, and what is
still missing.

### Megalodon THF Export

Status: usable, but final source binding still needs hardening.

The THF exporter can export current `aby` obligations, source names, origin
comments, and source-map information. It is good enough to produce large
hammer corpora and to run Vampire in parallel. It also records origin
information such as file, line, character, and obligation kind.

The export path still has delicate name and binder issues:

- local Megalodon variables become closed exported variables;
- generated names can be hash-prefixed;
- proof-local definitions shift de Bruijn indices;
- proposition-valued variables need special care when moved between exported
  THF and live Megalodon contexts;
- set-command facts may not correspond to user-written claims.

The exporter should eventually emit a source map whose semantics are directly
recoverable as Megalodon `Syntax.tm` values, not only as TPTP text and hashes.

### Vampire Search and Certificate Emission

Status: effective for finding proofs and printing metadata, not yet a clean
primitive proof-object layer.

Vampire can solve many exported THF problems under the required timeout and
emit Megalodon certificate data. The current exporter emits rule payloads for
many inference families. This has exposed the frequent classes in the real
corpus, including FOOL atom lifting, substitution, paramodulation,
superposition-related records, Skolemization, AVATAR split dependencies, and
predicate/definition transformations.

The main remaining Vampire-side design task is to normalize the proof into a
small primitive sequence. The current exporter is still too close to a dump of
special cases. The expert should look for whether future work is adding:

```text
typed primitive constructors with specified semantics
```

or merely:

```text
more fields consumed by Megalodon-side pattern matching
```

Only the first path is convergent.

### Certificate Parser and Structural Checker

Status: mature enough for integration evidence.

The Megalodon parser can parse the S-expression certificates, source maps,
primitive records, and rule-specific metadata. It has many positive and
negative smoke tests. It also has structural checks for source references and
metadata consistency.

This layer is valuable but not sufficient. A certificate that parses and
structurally validates still may not have its inferences reconstructed. That
is why structural `PASS` is E4.

### Source Resolver

Status: partial, with several real proof-producing cases.

The source resolver has moved beyond label matching. It can check known facts
in the loaded context and can prove some generated equalities by reflexivity.
It also tracks unresolved obligations. This is the right direction.

The unresolved part is a general source proof API. The design documents have
sketched something like:

```ocaml
type source_proof =
  | GlobalKnown of string * Syntax.tm
  | LocalHyp of int * Syntax.tm
  | Definitional of Syntax.tm * Syntax.pf
  | Generated of Syntax.tm * Syntax.pf
  | Conjecture of Syntax.tm
  | NegatedConjecture of Syntax.tm
```

The current code has related information but not yet a clean extracted API.
That extraction matters because final proof composition should consume source
proofs uniformly instead of embedding special cases throughout the importer.

### Native Clausal Kernel

Status: the most credible proof-term subsystem, but not fully extracted.

The native clausal kernel constructs proof terms for a small set of clausal
steps. It has synthetic and focused real fixtures. It has also uncovered
important binder-opening issues, especially when a stored step theorem is
opened under a later result context with retained, dropped, or substituted
variables.

The next core-kernel refactor should define a single theorem-opening operation
with an invariant. This should replace local fixes spread across resolution,
substitution, equality, and paramodulation code.

### Preprocessing Proof Layer

Status: promising but uneven.

Some preprocessing steps now have proof-term support. Others remain
structural-only or rely on focused implementations. This layer includes the
hardest parts of the project because THF/FOOL inputs are transformed before
Vampire's clausal proof begins.

The key design point is that preprocessing should not become Megalodon-side
guesswork. It should be a sequence of proof-producing transformations with
explicit data supplied by Vampire.

### Live `vampireaby` Composition

Status: first focused success exists, general solution incomplete.

The live path can export a current Megalodon goal, run Vampire, parse the
certificate, check relevant parts, and try to produce a proof payload. The
line-172 success shows that this can work in principle.

The next hard part is generic composition of exported conjectures with the
current local proof context. This includes:

- instantiating exported universal variables with live local variables;
- matching negated conjectures to current goals;
- handling CPS/double-negation shapes;
- using live library logic rather than certificate-private facts;
- ensuring no certificate-only Skolem or choice symbols remain in the final
  proof.

Line 183 is the active test for this layer.

## Trust Model

The trusted component is Megalodon's kernel/proof checker and the Megalodon
library facts already accepted in the current development. Vampire, the
certificate exporter, and the importer are untrusted. They may propose proof
terms, but those proof terms must be checked by Megalodon.

Therefore the following are not acceptable in a counted proof:

- `aby`;
- `admit`;
- `-allowincompleteqed`;
- bridge assumptions;
- derived Vampire clauses introduced as assumptions;
- dynamically installed certificate-derived `Known` facts;
- private equality or private classical axioms not resolved from the live
  Megalodon basis;
- theorem-specific hardwiring such as recognizing names like `and3I`, `P1`,
  `P2`, or `famunion`;
- Python-generated logical proof terms on the qualifying path.

The following are acceptable:

- using Vampire to find the proof;
- using Vampire to emit proof objects;
- using Megalodon OCaml code to elaborate certificate constructors into
  `Syntax.pf`;
- using `xm` and other classical facts already present in the live Megalodon
  development;
- using generated reflexivity proofs for set-command equality obligations;
- using Python for non-qualifying corpus analytics and log summarization.

## Frequent Inference Families

The real hammer corpus quickly reaches inference families beyond a tiny
textbook resolution kernel. The common classes include:

- FOOL atom lifting and proposition equality-to-truth conversions;
- substitution and rectification;
- paramodulation and superposition-style equality reasoning;
- Skolemization;
- predicate definition introduction/folding;
- CNF projection and literal extraction;
- AVATAR split dependencies and SAT-level refutations;
- URR and subsumption-related steps.

The project should not respond to this frequency table by adding all of those
as large Megalodon primitives. The correct response is to classify each
family:

```text
small clausal primitive
  resolution, factoring, equality resolution, equality factoring,
  paramodulation, substitution

macro to lower in Vampire
  superposition variants, demodulation, URR, subsumption where possible

separate transformation macro
  Skolemization, FOOL, ENNF, CNF, predicate definitions, rectification

separate propositional proof layer
  AVATAR SAT explanations
```

This classification should drive implementation. Otherwise the importer will
keep expanding around whichever class is currently failing.

## Line 172 Case Study

Line 172 is important because it is a focused original-context success. It is
not the final 100-proof result, but it proves that the architecture can cross
the full loop for at least one nontrivial live theorem:

```text
Megalodon source goal
  -> THF export
  -> Vampire certificate
  -> native certificate checking
  -> live basis transport
  -> proof payload accepted by Megalodon
```

The latest focused regression artifact is:

```text
/project/tmp/line172_epsilon_abs_regression_093612
```

The run reports that Vampire built a local `aby` proof candidate, produced a
proof payload, certified it, and stopped at the target after reconstructing
the proof term. This is the kind of evidence that should eventually be scaled
to 10 and then 100 examples.

The line-172 proof exercised more than a trivial propositional identity. It
interacted with Skolem/AVATAR replay and with live classical basis transport.
That is why it is a valuable regression.

The limitation is that one focused success does not imply generality. The
line-183 and line-233 failures show that the source-context and
Skolemization machinery is still incomplete.

## Line 183 Case Study

Line 183 is currently the best representative blocker because it is small
enough to inspect but rich enough to expose core issues. The theorem has only
proposition variables and conjunction, but the Vampire proof introduces FOOL
and Skolem/CPS machinery in a way that stresses the reconstruction pipeline.

The source theorem is:

```text
Section PropN.
Variable P1 P2 P3:prop.

Theorem and3I : P1 -> P2 -> P3 -> P1 /\ P2 /\ P3.
aby.
Qed.
```

The source-context issue is:

```text
Vampire certificate proposition:
  closed over exported proposition variables

Megalodon live goal:
  under local section variables P1, P2, P3

Needed:
  instantiate exported variables with live local variables, prove target
  converts to current goal, then apply refutation/CPS eliminator
```

The Skolem/CPS issue is:

```text
Vampire preprocessing introduces Skolem/epsilon witnesses.
The final Megalodon proof must not mention certificate-local witness symbols.
The checker must abstract exact witness terms under the right binders.
```

The latest committed branch does not solve the case. The uncommitted
experiment shows a useful transition:

```text
u30: native preprocess Skolem CPS discharged certificate-local witnesses
```

After that, the failure moves to proof-of-proposition composition. The most
informative error shape is:

```text
expected: ((iff A) true)
actual:   A -> true
```

or the inverse orientation in another candidate. That tells us that the next
work is a general FOOL/prop-ext/equality-to-truth conversion problem, not a
new Skolem witness problem.

This case should remain in the fast focused test suite. It should not be
solved by hardwiring names `P1`, `P2`, `P3`, or `and3I`.

## Line 233 and Explicit Skolem Substitution

Line 233 represents a different Skolemization problem. The earlier diagnostic
was:

```text
skolem_formula result does not match explicit skolem substitution
```

This is lower-level than the line-183 live composition failure. It means the
checker and Vampire's emitted Skolem substitution record disagree on the
result formula. This class needs negative tests and exact substitution
checking. It should not be papered over by formula normalization.

The correct fix is to specify the Skolem macro and make the checker verify:

```text
apply explicit substitution to source formula == emitted result formula
```

under the declared dependency context and binder convention.

## Relationship to Lean and Dedukti Export Modes

The original motivation included Vampire's Lean proof printing work. Lean and
Megalodon are morally similar in that both use proof terms. The user correctly
asked why this project did not begin by modifying the Lean output more
directly.

The answer is mixed:

- the Lean and Dedukti code showed that Vampire can emit proof-oriented data;
- the Megalodon implementation did use that idea, but initially drifted into
  Python and Megalodon-side reconstruction;
- the current S-expression/native certificate path is closer to a proper
  port of that idea, but it is still not a clean translation of the Lean
  extensions.

For future work, the expert should compare:

- Vampire's Lean/Dedukti output for resolution, superposition, demodulation,
  and preprocessing;
- the current Megalodon certificate fields for the same inferences;
- whether those fields are enough to build a proof term directly, without
  Megalodon-side inference guessing.

Where Lean/Dedukti already has a detailed proof expansion for an inference,
Megalodon should reuse the same Vampire-side structure whenever possible.

## Generality and Avoiding Library Overfitting

The implementation must reconstruct Vampire proofs over arbitrary Megalodon
libraries. It must not optimize for the current set-theory development.

Acceptable general mechanisms include:

- source-map resolution by hash or local context position;
- unmangling exported names by reversing the Megalodon TPTP mangling scheme;
- using type/sort declarations emitted by Vampire;
- checking formula conversion in the live Megalodon delta;
- using library facts by their loaded proof identifiers or verified
  propositions;
- reflexivity for generated definitional equalities;
- generic proof constructors for `and`, `or`, `iff`, equality, `False`, and
  `xm`.

Unacceptable overfitting includes:

- recognizing theorem names such as `famunion`;
- special cases for a particular file line except in a test harness;
- hardwired variable names such as `P1`, `P2`, `P3`;
- assuming set-library theorem shapes in the certificate checker;
- using Python or OCaml search patterns that encode particular library
  lemmas rather than certificate rules.

Some current debug code violates this if committed. The uncommitted
`native_core_pf_term_symbol_detail ["P1"; "P2"; "P3"]` query is acceptable as
a local diagnostic only. It must be removed or generalized before commit.

## Recommended Expert Review Questions

The expert reviewer should focus on these questions:

1. Is the proposed small kernel the right one for Vampire-to-Megalodon, or
   should equality/superposition be represented differently?

2. Which Vampire inferences should be lowered to primitive clausal steps, and
   which should remain separate macro certificates?

3. Is the Skolem macro design sufficient for Megalodon's classical basis, or
   should it use a different choice/epsilon encoding?

4. How should FOOL proposition equality-to-truth be represented so that final
   Megalodon proof terms use the live equality and `iff` basis cleanly?

5. What is the minimal source-context API needed to compose a closed exported
   refutation with a live local theorem goal?

6. How much of the current `src/vampire_cert_v1.ml` should be extracted, and
   what module boundaries would make the checker reviewable?

7. Should AVATAR be disabled for the first 10 or 100 E1 milestones, or should
   a tiny SAT certificate layer be introduced now?

8. Are the current proof-term checks sufficient to prevent certificate-derived
   known facts from returning under another name?

9. Should the first broad suite be selected from already-solvable THF
   problems, or should it start from a restricted Vampire schedule that avoids
   AVATAR and heavy preprocessing?

10. Which line-183 failure should be fixed first: proof-of-proposition
    equality-to-truth conversion, or guided conjecture instantiation? My
    current assessment is that both are required, but the equality-to-truth
    conversion is now the immediate post-Skolem blocker in the uncommitted
    experiment.

## Reproducibility Notes

The project has many `/project/tmp` artifacts. The most relevant current ones
are:

```text
/project/tmp/line172_epsilon_abs_regression_093612
/project/tmp/native_cert_v1.SHeiHE
/project/tmp/line183_close_depth_094632
/project/tmp/line183_term_abs_092646
/project/tmp/line183_unclosed_pnames_094117
```

The general focused command shape for live targets is:

```sh
work=/project/tmp/<run-name>
mkdir -p "$work/out"
TMPDIR=/project/tmp timeout 90s \
  /project/Megalodon/bin/megalodon \
  -v 4 \
  -allowincompleteqed \
  -vampireaby /project/vampire-leancheck/vampire_rel_vampire/megalodon5_11081 \
  -vampireabytimeout 10 \
  -vampireabyproof megalodon \
  -vampireabynative \
  -vampireabynativestrict \
  -vampireabytarget <line> 4 \
  -vampireabytargetstop \
  -vampireabyoutdir "$work/out" \
  /project/Megalodon/examples/hammer/100thms_12_h.mg \
  >"$work/run.out" 2>"$work/run.err"
```

The presence of `-allowincompleteqed` in this command is for running the
source file that still contains `aby` placeholders. It is not acceptable for
the final reconstructed proof. Counted success requires that the target proof
payload itself is reconstructed and accepted, not admitted.

For broad tests, the correct process is:

```text
1. Export a batch of problems once.
2. Run Vampire on the exported THF files in parallel, 10-20 workers.
3. Use a per-problem Vampire timeout <= 10 seconds.
4. Restrict reconstruction iteration to the once-discovered Vampire-solvable
   subset.
5. Check certificates/proofs in parallel.
6. Classify results by E1/E2/E3/E4, not by a single PASS label.
```

This avoids repeatedly paying Vampire portfolio cost for unsolved problems.

## Recommended Next Work

The next iteration should not pursue another broad pass-count increase.
It should be a controlled convergence step.

1. Clean the current uncommitted line-183 experiment.

   Remove theorem-specific debug code. Keep only the generic generated
   proposition closure if focused regressions show it is necessary and safe.
   Re-run `makeopt`, the smoke suite, line 172, and line 183. Commit only the
   general part.

2. Fix proof-of-proposition equality-to-true conversion.

   Implement a general live-basis proof constructor for:

   ```text
   A
   A -> true
   true -> A
   iff A true
   equality/prop-ext representations of A = true
   ```

   Use Megalodon's live equality, `iff`, `prop_ext`, and `xm` basis. Do not
   introduce a private equality theorem.

3. Implement guided source-context instantiation.

   Replace broad proposition enumeration with an operation that instantiates
   exported conjecture binders until the target converts to the current goal.

4. Specify the Skolem macro certificate.

   Write the expected Vampire fields, Megalodon proof-term elaboration, and
   negative tests before adding more Skolem cases.

5. Extract the native clausal kernel.

   Move kernel syntax, theorem opening, substitution application, and proof
   elaboration into smaller modules. Define a shared operation:

   ```ocaml
   open_step_theorem :
     global_context ->
     parent_step_variables ->
     result_step_variables ->
     substitution ->
     stored_proof ->
     opened_proof
   ```

6. Move more detail to Vampire, but through a typed primitive builder.

   Do not add arbitrary fields just because the current Megalodon checker is
   missing something. Add fields only as part of a small primitive constructor

7. Build a small committed E1 suite before broad testing.

   A good next milestone is not "100 structural passes." It is 10 committed,

   expand to 100.

## Outlook

The project is still viable, but only if it continues the architectural reset.

The positive case is real:

- Vampire can already find many THF proofs quickly;
- the source maps and origin metadata now expose the original Megalodon
  development structure;
- Megalodon can now check native proof terms for nontrivial certificate
  fragments;
- line 172 shows that original-context reconstruction is not merely
  hypothetical;
- the line-183 experiment shows that the current Skolem/CPS witness problem is
  tractable and that the next blocker is more sharply localized.

The risk is also real:

- broad Megalodon-side OCaml reconstruction can become another theorem prover;
- Vampire-side metadata can become a string dump rather than a proof object;
- benchmark-driven patches can overfit to the current library;
- proof-of-proposition and source-context composition can absorb time unless
  they are specified as small general operations.

The best route is to treat the current branch as a valuable prototype and
regression corpus, then keep extracting the real architecture from it:

```text
Vampire primitive expansion
  + small typed certificate
  + Megalodon native Syntax.pf checker
  + explicit source-context proof resolver
  + specified Smolka-style transformation macros
```

The project should continue, but its next success criterion should be narrow
and auditable:

```text
10 original-context, source-bound, no-admit, no-aby Megalodon proofs checked
from Vampire THF certificates, with all intermediate proof terms accepted by
Megalodon and no certificate-derived Known facts.
```

Once that is stable and committed, scaling to 100 becomes a meaningful
engineering goal rather than a broad search over a moving prototype.
